# Data sources

`word-properties.js` is generated by `tools/build-dictionary.mjs` (run with
`npm run build:dictionary`). It is committed so the app works offline with no
build step, as the original program did.

The word list itself, `dictionary.js`, came with this project and derives from
the original Anagram Artist for Windows. Everything else is reconstructed from
the open datasets below, because the original's dictionary format carried
commonness, part of speech, syllables, stress, pronunciation and synonyms that
a bare word list does not.

## Sources

| Property | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Syllables, stress pattern, rhyme keys | [CMU Pronouncing Dictionary](https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict) (`cmudict.dict`) | BSD 2-Clause |
| Parts of speech, synonyms | [Princeton WordNet 3.1](https://wordnet.princeton.edu/) (`wn3.1.dict.tar.gz`) | WordNet 3.0 Licence (permissive, attribution required) |
| Commonness ranks | [hackerb9/gwordlist](https://github.com/hackerb9/gwordlist) `frequency-alpha-alldicts.txt`, derived from the Google Books Ngram corpus | CC BY 3.0 |

WordNet attribution, as its licence requires:

> Princeton University "About WordNet." WordNet. Princeton University. 2010.

## Coverage

Of the 89,759 words in the dictionary, 82,562 (92%) carry at least one property:

| Property | Words | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Part of speech | 71,209 | 79% |
| Commonness rank | 61,656 | 69% |
| Pronunciation (syllables, stress, rhyme) | 43,216 | 48% |
| Synonyms | 25,825 | 29% |

The gaps are real and are left as gaps. The dictionary is a large international
word list containing many rare and inflected forms that these sources do not
cover; a word missing from a source has no value for that property, and the
filters treat it as unknown. Nothing is inferred to fill a hole.

Two consequences worth knowing:

- **Pronunciation coverage is the weakest.** CMUdict is American and does not
  include most inflected or obscure forms, so the syllable, stress and rhyme
  filters will see roughly half the dictionary.
- **Interjections are unavailable.** The original has six part-of-speech
  buttons; WordNet classifies only four (noun, verb, adjective, adverb) and has
  no interjection data. The `Int` button is disabled and says so on hover.
  `Oth` means "not classified by WordNet as any of the four".

Part-of-speech coverage is raised from 47% to 79% by WordNet's own detachment
rules ("Morphy") plus its exception lists, which resolve inflected forms such as
`dowsers` to the lemma `dowser`. A candidate only counts when the resulting
lemma is actually present in the WordNet index.

## A note on the dictionary's spelling-variant markers

2,567 of the 89,759 words carry an internal capital letter: `coSy`/`cozY`,
`armoR`/`armoUr`, `gaOl`/`jaiL`, `JudaiSe`/`JudaizE`. The capital marks the
letter that distinguishes the pair. There is always exactly one, never in first
position, and no two words collide once it is removed.

That marker is data, not spelling, so `normalizeDictionaryWord` in `analysis.js`
lowercases everything after the first character before the word is displayed or
looked up. Genuine leading capitals (`Ottawa`, `I`, `O` - 1,117 of them) are
preserved.

**What the markers are for.** `toolbar.txt` explains them, under Settings /
Dictionary: the original could restrict suggestions to American spelling,
British spelling, or both, and "knows about roughly 1300 pairs of words spelled
differently in American English and British English". 2,567 marked words is
1,283 pairs, which matches. So the marker is the original's spelling-variant
mechanism.

Two consequences:

- The American/British/Both setting is built on these markers.
  `classifySpellingVariant` in `analysis.js` reads the marker, then matches the
  normalised word against a small set of American and British spelling-rule
  patterns to decide which side of a pair it is on; against the shipped
  dictionary that classifies 1,270 words American and 1,291 British out of the
  2,567 marked (the rest are genuinely shared spellings). "Am" or "Br" in the
  Settings dialog keeps words of that spelling plus words with no variant
  data; "Both" (the default) passes everything.
- The 71 American spellings added in `dictionary-additions.js` carry no marker
  capital, so they are treated as "both" and survive either filter rather than
  being pinned to one side of a pair.

## Supplementary words

`dictionary.js` is left exactly as it came from the original program, so its
provenance stays intact. Words the original omits go in
`dictionary-additions.js` instead, each with a reason, so every departure from
the original data is visible and reversible.

Additions are merged at load time and are read by `build-dictionary.mjs`, so
they receive the same commonness, part-of-speech, syllable, stress, rhyme and
synonym data as everything else. Listing a word the original already has is a
no-op, so entries need no checking first.

Currently added:

| Word | Reason |
|---|---|
| `American` | The original has America, Americas, Americana, Americans, Americanism and Americanise/ize, but not the singular. The gap also swallowed the exact anagram of AMERICAN. |

## Commonness buckets

The original's bar has five segments, `1 2 3 4 All`, where "Subset 1 consists of
just the most common words (about 10% of the dictionary). The next subset
(segments 1 and 2 combined) is even bigger (about 25%) and so on"
(`filters.txt`). Ranks are therefore assigned as percentiles of *this
dictionary*, not of the source corpus: 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%. Words beyond that, or
absent from the frequency data, are only visible at `All`.

## Refreshing

```bash
npm run build:dictionary
```

Sources are cached in `tools/cache/` (gitignored); delete it to re-download.
